Delivery principles

Principle 01
Discovery and detailed scoping is paramount
What this means in practice

A clear documentation of the exact scope of build (glue jobs, Python scripts, API calls), alongside collection of all existing artefacts including code, scripts, and documentation.

Historical numbers are agreed with source systems and business stakeholders upfront, giving the team a true baseline before development begins.

We also align on key reporting requirements, both current and future state, to ensure all required data is in scope from day one.

Principle 02
Controls come before content
What this means in practice

Version control, CI, environment promotion, and rollback land in week one, before any build begins.

Assessing completed work and deciding what to retain, enhance, or rebuild requires a defensible baseline. Without version history from the outset, that assessment cannot be made with confidence.

Principle 03
End-to-end orchestration working first
What this means in practice

We work through dependencies, lineage, CI/CD DevOps framework, and medallion architecture before expanding scope, setting the data up for success from the start.

This proves the pattern, gives the business something tangible early, and turns repeatability into a demonstrated template rather than an assumption.

02 · Data layer architecture and the missing conformance stage

There are five processing stages, each with a single well defined responsibility. RAW provides source faithful landing with ingest audit columns, giving a verifiable baseline for dispute resolution. ODS is a full history, date stamped operational data store with CDC applied and quality quarantine. INTEGRATION handles cross entity conformance, covering canonical codes, FX and unit of measure. EDW is Kimball modelled, with reporting grain facts and additive measures. SEM is the governed semantic layer, holding ratios, derived metrics, period comparisons and entitlement scoping.

The INTEGRATION layer does not exist today, and it is the most commercially consequential gap. This is where each company's account codes would be mapped to a single group chart of accounts within the platform, versioned and owner attributed, with an exception report for unmapped values. It would also maintain the canonical entity register, covering acquisitions, disposals and name changes with effective dates, so that a mid year acquisition consolidates correctly without manual report reconstruction. Moving chart of accounts conformance from downstream spreadsheets into the platform turns a reporting database into a consolidation system.

INTEGRATION is also the source coupling boundary. Above it, all objects reference the group's canonical schema rather than any individual ERP's. This decoupling matters structurally, since [Client Name] was evaluating a [finance/ERP] system replacement and continues to grow running disparate ERPs. With the conformance boundary in place, replacing or onboarding a source system becomes a re-connection exercise rather than a downstream rebuild.

03 · Semantic layer: a versioned metric register with dual-surface generation

The semantic layer would be implemented as a versioned metric register, a governed catalogue where each metric carries its business definition, calculation formula, exclusion rules, entity scope, currency and calendar treatment, ownership, version and approval record. The register is the primary deliverable, and the technology surfaces are its implementation. It is the artefact the business signs, and it becomes the single authority for what "group EBITDA" resolves to across the platform.

From this register, the platform compiles two serving surfaces: Snowflake semantic views, consumed by Cortex Analyst, CoCo and SQL based tools, and governed presentation views, consumed by Power BI. Both are generated from one dbt managed definition set, and neither is hand maintained. A CI enforced parity gate compares output values on every change, and if a metric diverges between the two surfaces, the deployment is blocked. This is designed to remove the failure mode where the board pack and an AI assistant silently disagree.

Two consequences follow from this design. First, the AI objective in scope becomes achievable precisely because the semantic layer governs it, since Cortex Analyst would query the same definitions that produce the board pack, rather than operating against ungoverned raw tables that could create a competing version of the truth. Second, we recommend not renewing the [Incumbent Semantic Tool] licence (~[LICENCE COST] a year) and building the semantic capability natively in Snowflake. That recommendation is conditional on three validation checks that can be completed within two weeks of mobilisation, with a named fallback if any check does not hold.

You asked for a view and a recommendation. In your case this question has a clear answer, because your own delivery history points firmly in one direction.

In favour: future questions are answerable without returning to the source, each source is handled in a single pass, repeated negotiation with source owners is avoided, it supports later data science work, and it offers a simpler mental model.

Why AI matters most here, and where it does not

In our view the highest-value application of AI on this engagement is not code generation. It is reverse-documenting an undocumented estate before the people who understand it move on.   • Code generation. Genuinely faster for models, tests and boilerplate. Bounded, because review rather than authoring becomes the constraint and we do not shorten review.
